I've been solving CTFs, breaking things in labs, and taking notes for a while now. Most of those notes lived in private markdown files, scattered screenshots, and half-finished documents. This blog is where they become useful to someone other than me.

## Why a blog?

Writing forces clarity. A challenge that feels "obvious" in the moment often has three hidden assumptions that only surface when you try to explain it. Turning a solve into a writeup is the last step of actually understanding it.

There's also a selfish reason: when I solve something interesting six months from now, I want to be able to search my own archive instead of re-deriving everything from scratch.

## What you'll find here

I plan to publish three kinds of posts:

- **CTF writeups** — full walkthroughs of challenges I found interesting, with the dead ends included, not just the clean final exploit.
- **Security research** — deeper dives into vulnerabilities, tooling, and things I run into while building web applications.
- **Notes** — shorter posts on workflows, study plans, and lessons learned.

## How the posts are organized

Every post has a category and a set of tags. Categories are broad:

| Category | What it covers |
| --- | --- |
| CTF Writeup | Challenge walkthroughs from events and practice platforms |
| Security Research | Vulnerability analysis, tooling, and web security deep dives |
| Notes | Shorter posts on process and learning |

Tags are specific: `web`, `pwn`, `crypto`, `active-directory`, `burp`, and so on. You can search and filter from the [blog index](/blog).

## A note on responsible disclosure

For anything involving a real target, I follow the usual rules: stay in scope, don't touch other people's data, and disclose privately before publishing. Everything here is either from a CTF, a deliberately vulnerable lab, or a system I have explicit permission to test.
